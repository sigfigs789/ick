# Drop hints — draft

A hint teaches a way to look for words, not a word. It should still be useful
the next time the same drop comes round, and on drops it was never written for.

Two rules held throughout:

- **Name the drop, use an imperative.** No bare "here" or "there" — the player
  sees three letters in a box, so "the words worth finding don't end here" has
  no referent on screen.
- **Illustrate with the trap, never the payoff.** Every word named in a hint is
  a common one the player was going to reach for anyway. Naming `cake` costs
  nothing; naming the rare answer is the old reveal in a new costume.

## The general hints

These work on any drop. Evidence below says these should be the bulk of what
ships.

1. **The rarest words usually bury the drop in the middle.** Not at the start,
   not at the end.
2. **Join two words you already know.** The drop often falls on the seam.
3. **Run the alphabet in front of the drop, out loud.** A, then B, then C.
   Checking a list beats waiting for a word to arrive.
4. **Put a prefix on it** — a prefix makes a new word, where a plural or an
   `-ing` does not.
5. **Pick a room and stand in it.** A kitchen, a hospital, a workshop.
   Vocabulary is filed by context, so a scene pulls up a shelf where a blank
   search pulls up nothing.
6. **Ask who says this word.** A chef, a sailor, a botanist. Every trade has
   words the rest of us never use.
7. **Say it, don't define it.** Sound-based recall reaches different words than
   meaning-based recall.
8. **Skip the first word that comes to mind.** It is almost always a common one.

## Why general, and not per-drop

The rarest words concentrate in one position for 73 of the 100 drops surveyed —
but **58 of those 73 say "the middle"**. A per-drop hint would be the same
sentence nearly every time, so the specificity buys almost nothing.

Drop-specific copy only earns its place where a drop *departs* from the general
rule:

**The rarest words end with these** — hint 1 is wrong here, so say so:

| drop | rarest words ending with it |
| --- | --- |
| TLY | 99% |
| IES | 92% |
| TS | 91% |
| VED | 86% |
| SES | 84% |
| ERS | 81% |
| KS | 78% |
| RS | 74% |
| ORS | 72% |
| ONS | 71% |
| NDS | 67% |

**The rarest words start with these:** ACC (71%), PRO (70%), COM (70%),
REP (68%).

Everything else takes the general rotation.

## Phrasing

State where the rarest words are, rather than what the player is doing wrong:

> Rarest words bury ILL in the middle.
> Rarest words end with TLY.

This reads as a fact about the puzzle rather than a correction, and it fixes the
missing-referent problem by naming the drop.

**It also raises the evidence bar**, because a flat claim has to be true. Two
worked examples of claims that failed the check:

- *"Rarest words don't end with AKE"* — false. 10% of the rare pool ends there
  against 20% of the common pool. Real difference, but the flat claim overstates
  it.
- *"Rarest words don't start with ACC"* — false. 68% of them do; they are merely
  less likely to than common words (87%).

Where the gap is a tendency rather than an absolute, the honest phrasing is
comparative, and comparatives are wordier and weaker. Prefer drops where the
absolute claim holds.

## What is not settled

**Placement.** Whether the hint appears during play or after completion is still
open, and it changes the writing: during play it is a hint, after completion it
is a lesson.

**Measurement.** Position was first surveyed on raw Zipf, which measures rarity
in English, not the folded score the game shows. Re-running 23 drops on folded
scores erased most of the difference between rare and common placement:

| drop | raw delta | folded delta |
| --- | --- | --- |
| LIS | +18 | −1 |
| ERE | +19 | 0 |
| URE | +29 | 0 |
| AGE | +23 | 0 |
| PRO | +16 | −9 |
| AKE | +40 | +7 |

Only ACC, JO, PRI, GRA, ILL, NDS and TIC kept a meaningful gap. The general
hints above do not depend on that gap — they describe where rare words sit, not
how they differ from common ones — which is a further argument for leading with
them. The remaining 77 drops have not been re-surveyed on folded scores.
